Everyone starts somewhere. Employers hiring for first jobs and internships know you don’t have years of experience — they want to see that you can learn, work with people and get things done. Your studies, projects, part-time work and activities already show that.

What to put on your first resume

  1. Contact details — name, phone, email, city, and a LinkedIn or GitHub link if you have one.
  2. A short summary (2–3 lines) — who you are and what job you want.
  3. Education — put it first when you have little experience. Include your degree, college, year and good marks if they help.
  4. Projects — college projects, personal projects, competitions, a website you built, a business idea you tried.
  5. Experience of any kind — internships, part-time jobs, volunteering, tutoring, family business, club leadership.
  6. Skills — software, languages, tools and practical skills the job asks for.
  7. Certificates and awards — online courses, prizes, sports or cultural achievements.

Write a summary that works

Keep it short and specific. Say what you study, one or two strengths, and the role you want.

Example: “Final-year commerce student with internship experience in bookkeeping and strong MS Excel skills. Organised, quick to learn and looking for a junior accountant role.”

Turn activities into achievements

Start each line with an action word and add a number whenever you can. Compare:

WeakStrong
Was part of the college fest teamOrganised a 2-day college fest for 1,200 students with a team of 15
Did a project on attendanceBuilt a face-recognition attendance app used by 300 students
Helped in my uncle’s shopHandled billing and stock for a family shop serving 80+ customers a day

Match each resume to the job

Many companies use software that scans resumes for the skills in the job advert. Read the advert and use the same words — if it asks for “MS Excel” and “customer service” and you have those skills, write them exactly like that. In our resume builder, paste the job advert into “Match it to a job” to see which keywords you are missing.

Keep it to one page

  • One page is perfect for freshers. Recruiters often spend less than a minute on a first look.
  • Use a clean, simple design. Single-column designs are the safest for company job portals.
  • Choose a professional email address — firstname.lastname@… rather than a nickname.
  • Check spelling twice, and ask a friend to read it.
